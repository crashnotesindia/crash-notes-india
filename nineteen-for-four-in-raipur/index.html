Nineteen for four in Raipur: the night Mandhana's captaincy started with a scare

On paper, the first T20I in Raipur on 16 October was one of the most lopsided fixtures of the season. India Women had won eight T20Is in a row. Zimbabwe had lost all 18 they had played in 2026. Smriti Mandhana was leading India for the first time as full-time captain after the BCCI named her on 6 October, with Harmanpreet Kaur still in the squad. Nineteen balls into the chase, India were 19 for 4.

A modest total that did its job

Mandhana won the toss and bowled. Zimbabwe made 114 for 5, built around Sharne Mayers with 43 not out from 33 balls and Kelis Ndhlovu with 42 from 44. Nandani Sharma conceded 11 runs in her four overs and took two wickets. Shree Charani went for 14. A target of 115 should have been a gentle evening, and for six balls it was.

Where the chase went wrong

Off-spinner Nyasha Gwanzura removed Mandhana for 9, Anushka Sharma for 4 at No. 3 and Shafali Verma for 4, finishing with 3 for 18. Michelle Mavunga had Harmanpreet for 1. Four of India's most experienced batters were gone before the powerplay ended. That is the part of the night I keep thinking about, because it says more about a slow Raipur surface than about any gap in class.

Two batters who did not panic

Niki Prasad, playing her first T20I, made 39 not out from 35 balls. Deepti Sharma made 49 from 37 and was named player of the match. Their stand of 82 took the heat out of the game, and India got home at 116 for 6 in 15.4 overs with 26 balls to spare. A four-wicket win against a side that had lost 18 straight is not a crisis. It still showed how quickly an easy chase can tighten on a pitch that grips.

Two days later India won the second game by nine wickets to go 2-0 up, and Prasad took 2 for 7 in it. Mandhana will still remember her first 19 balls in charge.
