Two nights at Seddon Park, and why I never trust a dead rubber in Hamilton

When people call the last match of a won series a dead rubber, I think of Hamilton. India have played only two T20Is at Seddon Park, and both ended in the final over or beyond it. So when this month's fifth T20I came round with India already 3-1 up, I did not treat it as a formality.

The timing in India

An 8:00 PM local start in Hamilton is a Sunday lunch game for us. New Zealand runs seven and a half hours ahead in summer, so the toss lands around noon IST and the first ball at 12:30 PM. The chase finishes around 4 PM if the overs move on time. In our house that meant rice, dal and the commentary turned up a little too loud.

February 2019

The first of those two matches was a series decider. New Zealand made 212 for 4, with Colin Munro hitting 72 from 40 balls. India looked out of it until Dinesh Karthik and Krunal Pandya put on 63 for the seventh wicket. They took it to the last ball and lost by four runs. I remember standing up during the final over and sitting down again without saying anything.

January 2020

The second was even tighter. India made 179, Rohit Sharma scored 65, and Kane Williamson's 95 from 48 balls nearly won it alone. Mohammed Shami bowled Ross Taylor off the last ball to tie the scores, and then Rohit hit the last two balls of the Super Over for six. It was the first Super Over India had ever played in international cricket.

Before Sunday I read the Hamilton 5th T20I page with the IST hours of play and India's two earlier T20Is at Seddon Park, and it made the same point I had in my head. Two games is a thin record, but both say that on this small ground 180 is a par score, not a big one, and games go to the end.

What a won series changes

The other thing the page is honest about is rotation. With the trophy already secured after Abhishek Sharma's 35-ball hundred in Wellington, and the ODIs only three days away, both sides had reasons to rest players. That makes every view formed on the first four games shaky. A top-scorer idea built on Abhishek collapses if he sits out or bats lower to give Ishan Kishan a look.

My own rule for a final game is now simple. Wait for the team sheets, about half an hour before the start, before having any opinion at all. And in Hamilton, do not switch the television off early.

Real-money online betting is banned in India under the 2025 Act; for me cricket is for watching, arguing over and remembering.
