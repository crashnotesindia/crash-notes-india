One endorsement, two rulebooks: what a cricket star's betting deal means for a fan in India

In mid-October a cricket WhatsApp group I am in lit up with a clip of Kevin Pietersen, who had just become a global brand ambassador for Dafabet. Half the group thought it was funny. One member, a lawyer in Kochi, posted a single line under it: "Please do not forward this." She had a point, and it is worth explaining why.

The English side of the story

Pietersen announced the deal on 12 October. According to The Guardian, he told the England and Wales Cricket Board two days later that he would not promote Dafabet while working as England's specialist mentor, a role of around 150 days over the next 13 months. The ECB's anti-corruption code bars anyone inside English cricket from encouraging another person to bet on any part of a match. The board has seen this before. Brendon McCullum promoted 22bet's IPL markets a few years ago, was found not to have broken the code, and ended the arrangement anyway.

Dafabet holds a licence to operate in Britain. That licence has weight in Britain. It does not carry any in an Indian dispute.

The Indian side of the story

India's Promotion and Regulation of Online Gaming Act, 2025 and its rules have been in force since 1 May 2026. Section 6 says no person shall make, aid or be involved in any advertisement that directly or indirectly promotes an online money game, in any media. The penalty under Section 9(2) for a first offence is up to two years, a fine of up to ₹50 lakh, or both. A repeat offence can mean up to three years and a fine up to ₹1 crore. Offering such a game, or processing payments for one, carries up to three years and ₹1 crore for a first offence.

The Act's own statement of purpose names "celebrity and influencer endorsements" as part of the problem. India has already seen the Enforcement Directorate attach ₹11.14 crore of assets linked to Suresh Raina and Shikhar Dhawan in November 2025, in its case over the 1xBet brand and its surrogate names.

What this means for a fan with a phone

None of the reporting says Pietersen's deal is aimed at India, and nobody has accused him of anything here. The practical points are narrower. A tips video or giveaway from a betting brand is still an advert, whoever presents it. Forwarding a referral link or code from a money-gaming site to a group is the riskiest version. Look-alike names are a warning sign.
