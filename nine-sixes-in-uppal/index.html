Nine sixes in Uppal: how Shai Hope pulled the series back to 2-2

India went to Hyderabad on 14 October one win away from the T20I series. They left it level at 2-2, after West Indies made 247 for 3 and won by 24 runs. I had expected a high score at Uppal. I had not expected India to lose a chase that began at 50 without loss inside three overs.

The pitch everyone warned about

The Rajiv Gandhi International Stadium had been relaid, and the recent numbers were frightening for bowlers: T20 totals of 310, 297, 286 and 277 in two years. India won the toss and chose to field, which made sense on paper. Captains on this tour wanted to chase under lights, with dew expected after 9 PM.

Shai Hope made the decision look wrong. He scored 102 not out off 50 balls with nine sixes, his third T20I hundred, and the first time a West Indies batter had reached three. Sherfane Rutherford added 49 not out off 26. Prince Yadav was the only Indian bowler who kept it under control, with 1 for 36.

A chase that looked on track

Sanju Samson hit 56 off 23 and for a few overs the target of 248 looked ordinary. Then Keemo Paul came back. He took 5 for 41, and Gudakesh Motie's two overs cost only 13 runs for two wickets. Tilak Varma made 43 off 25 and Nitish Kumar Reddy 41 off 20, enough to keep India in it until Paul's 19th over, when three wickets fell. India were all out for 223 in 19.3 overs.

Arshdeep Singh retiring hurt after a blow to the bicep did not help an attack that was already without Jasprit Bumrah.

Where the series stood afterwards

The run of results was strange. India chased easily in Lucknow, West Indies chased 250 in Ranchi, India defended 223 in Indore, and now West Indies had defended 247. Four games, four different stories. The decider went to Bengaluru on 17 October.

The lesson from Uppal is an old one. On a flat ground a good start in a chase is worth less than wickets in hand at the 15-over mark.
