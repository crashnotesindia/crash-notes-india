The decider at Lincoln: India A's last red-ball audition before Wellington

Two matches, two draws. One game left. The third unofficial Test between New Zealand A and India A starts on Saturday 7 November at Bert Sutcliffe Oval in Lincoln, the same ground where the series began. It is also the last red-ball cricket India's fringe Test players get before the first Test in Wellington on 19 November.

Early starts for fans in India

Play is set for 11:00 AM local time on each of the four days. With New Zealand on daylight saving time, that is 3:30 AM IST from Saturday 7 to Tuesday 10 November. Lunch is around 5:30 AM IST, tea around 8:10 AM and stumps near 10:30 AM, later if rain or bad light take time out. On the final day of the second match, a rain delay held play back until 5:10 AM IST.

How the first two games went

The opening match in Lincoln nearly produced a result. India A were bowled out for 113 on the first morning, with Simon Keene taking 5 for 24. They fought back with 516 in the second innings, built on Devdutt Padikkal's 199 and a stand of 215 with Sai Sudharsan. Set 313, New Zealand A slipped to 13 for 3 and survived at 187 for 9, thanks to an unbeaten 90 from Curtis Heaphy.

Christchurch swung the other way. Captain Dhruv Jurel made 105 to lift India A to 346 from 160 for 7. Matthew Boyle and Dean Foxcroft then put on 208 for New Zealand A, who took a lead of 78. India A lost two early wickets in reply, and then Yashasvi Jaiswal, 155 not out, and Yash Rathod, 100 not out, batted through the last day in an unbroken stand of 247.

Who to watch

Jaiswal arrives with the strongest claim. The bowlers are under more pressure. Mukesh Kumar took 5 for 87 in Christchurch and Mohammed Siraj 4 for 118, on a pitch that gave little help. Nitish Kumar Reddy joins the squad after the first senior ODI, and Prasidh Krishna comes in if he passes a fitness test. Both would add pace and a chance to show the selectors they are ready for Wellington.

For New Zealand A, Foxcroft is worth following before he moves into the senior ODI squad, and Keene showed on the first morning how dangerous the Lincoln pitch can be with a new ball.
