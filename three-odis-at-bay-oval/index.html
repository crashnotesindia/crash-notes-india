Three ODIs at Bay Oval, and what they taught me about chasing by the sea

Cricket in New Zealand means breakfast cricket for us. When India played the fourth ODI of this month's series at Bay Oval, the toss was around 7:00 AM IST and the first ball at 7:30 AM, which in our house means chai, toast and the radio commentary competing with the pressure cooker.

Before that match I went back over India's history at the ground, because it is one of the few New Zealand venues where an Indian side has a winning record.

A short record, mostly good

Bay Oval in Mount Maunganui only became an international ground in 2014, so there are not many matches to go on. India have played three ODIs against New Zealand there. In January 2019 Rohit Sharma made 87 off 96 balls in an opening stand of 154 with Shikhar Dhawan, and Kuldeep Yadav took 4 for 45 in a 90-run win. Two days later Mohammed Shami took 3 for 41, Virat Kohli made 60, and India chased for a seven-wicket win.

The third match is the one I remember best. In February 2020 KL Rahul scored 112 from 113 balls and India still lost, with New Zealand chasing 297 down with 17 balls to spare to finish a 3-0 sweep.

Why the toss matters there

A Mount Maunganui preview that walks through India's three ODIs against New Zealand at Bay Oval made a point I had not thought about. These are day/night games that start at 3:00 PM local time. The side batting second gets the cooler evening air and any dew, which helps batting and makes the ball harder for spinners to grip. Captains here have often chosen to chase. The ground has a fast outfield and a breeze off the Pacific that can help swing early in the evening.

That same preview set out the changes for the last two ODIs: Shubman Gill and KL Rahul leaving to prepare for the Tests, Shreyas Iyer leading the side, Ishan Kishan as vice-captain, and uncapped wicketkeeper Kumar Kushagra joining the squad. New Zealand's group was very different too, with Mitchell Santner still captain and Finn Allen and Lockie Ferguson bringing pace.

What I take from the old scorecards

Three matches are not a pattern you can bet a house on. But they do say something useful. Rohit, Kohli and Kuldeep had all won an ODI at this ground before, which is rare for any Indian team in New Zealand. And the 2020 match is a reminder that a century from one batter does not settle a flat pitch with a quick outfield.

When I watch a match there now, I look at the toss first, the dew second and the scorecard only third. The timing in IST is the easy part: set the alarm for seven.
