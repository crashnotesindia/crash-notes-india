A car, a road and a Range button: reading CarX without the Aviator label

Most crash games copy the same picture: something flies upward and you leave before it disappears. CarX from SmartSoft keeps the multiplier and swaps the sky for a road. A car speeds along, the number climbs, and the round ends when the car stops. That small change of theme is enough to make people call it "Aviator with a car", which is not accurate.

Two kinds of bet, not one

The part that actually differs is the bet menu. SmartSoft's product page lists two categories. Cash Out is the familiar one: you press before the car stops and take your stake times the multiplier. Range is different. You choose a band in advance and bet that the round will end inside it.

So a session can look like this. ₹100 on Cash Out, aimed at a modest 1.40x, which would return ₹140. Another ₹100 on a Range you think is likely. Two tickets on one car. Neither of them gives you an edge you have not read on a rules screen, but the two rails do change how quickly a budget moves.

Which numbers come from where

When I checked, the studio page did not print an RTP or a top multiplier. Aggregator sites fill that gap. One catalogue lists 98.9 percent RTP and a 25,000x maximum. Those figures may match the game, but they are not a studio certificate, and an operator can configure its own cap. The help panel inside the client is the place to confirm them.

Names that cause confusion

Search results in India mix CarX up with three other things. Aviator is Spribe's plane game. JetX and Crash Duel X are SmartSoft titles with their own maths sheets. And CarX Street is a racing simulator with no betting at all. If a page credits a different studio or shows a lobby screenshot that looks edited, I treat it as a name trap until the provider line reads SmartSoft.

Try the demo before anything else

SmartSoft offers a free play-money demo on its own site. Five minutes with it shows you how fast the car stops on an average round, which matters more than any headline multiplier.
