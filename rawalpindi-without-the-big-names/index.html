Rawalpindi without the big names: how the Pakistan v Sri Lanka series opener looked before the toss

The first T20I between Pakistan and Sri Lanka at Rawalpindi Cricket Stadium on Friday 9 October 2026 was an odd fixture to preview. Both teams turned up short of their best-known players, and for Indian viewers it began at 6:30 PM IST, half an hour before India and West Indies started in Ranchi. Looking back at what was known before the toss is a good lesson in how much a preview can and cannot tell you.

Who was missing

Pakistan named their 15 on 5 October without Babar Azam or Shaheen Shah Afridi, both kept for the ODIs. Salman Ali Agha, Usman Khan and the spinner Usman Tariq were also out. Sahibzada Farhan, who had led the side at the Asian Games, stayed captain. Razaullah earned a first white-ball call-up and Muhammad Imran Randhawa a first T20I selection.

Sri Lanka were without Pathum Nissanka, with a right-leg strain, and Wanindu Hasaranga, with a back problem. That removed their most reliable opener and their best T20 wrist-spinner at once. Kusal Mendis led, arriving with 198 runs in three ODIs in England, including 121. Dushmantha Chameera was listed "subject to fitness".

What the ground had said a year earlier

The two sides had played three T20Is at Rawalpindi in the November 2025 tri-series. Pakistan won two, including the final, and the side batting second won two of the three. Sri Lanka made 184 there and then 114 two days later, which is a fair warning against reading too much into one first-innings total. Chameera had been player of the match in Sri Lanka's win, so his fitness carried extra weight.

Form and spin

Pakistan's last T20 had been the Asian Games final on 3 October, which India won by 19 runs after making 211 for 6; Hasan Nawaz made 96 in the chase. Abrar Ahmed had gone to No. 1 in the ICC T20I bowling rankings on 23 September, with Shadab Khan and Sufiyan Moqim alongside him.

How the price read

The clearest record of all this is the Rawalpindi 1st T20I preview that went through both squads and the ground's 2025 results. It turned the public average prices of 1.42 for Pakistan and 2.75 for Sri Lanka into implied chances of about 66% and 34% once the bookmakers' margin was removed. A clear favourite, then, and still a side expected to lose about one game in three. It also flagged the things that could move the number: dew, Chameera's fitness and which two of Farhan, Saim Ayub and Fakhar Zaman would open.

Why it is worth rereading

A good preview is a list of open questions, written before anyone knows the answers. Reading it after the event shows which questions mattered. For readers in India, where real-money online betting is banned under the Promotion and Regulation of Online Gaming Act, 2025, that is the useful way to read odds: as a measure of opinion before the toss, for adults following the game.
